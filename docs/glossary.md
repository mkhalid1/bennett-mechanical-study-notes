# Glossary

Plain definitions of the terms used in these notes. The physics terms are standard. The scoring terms follow Pearson's descriptions on its [BMCT-II page](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| BMCT-II | The Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test, second edition, published by Pearson TalentLens |
| Centre of gravity | The point where an object's weight can be treated as acting. An object tips when its centre of gravity moves past the edge of its base. |
| Conduction | Heat moving through a solid, from hot parts to cold parts. Metals conduct well. Wood, plastic and air conduct poorly. |
| Convection | Heat carried by a moving liquid or gas. Warm air and water rise, cooler air and water sink. |
| Current | The flow of electric charge round a circuit, measured in amps (A) |
| Echo | Sound reflected back from a surface. Its delay covers the distance there and back. |
| Gear ratio | How many turns the driven gear makes for each turn of the driver. Teeth x rpm is the same on both gears. |
| Hydraulic press | A machine that uses trapped liquid to multiply force. The force grows by the ratio of the piston areas. |
| Idler gear | A gear between two others. It changes direction but not the overall speed ratio. |
| Inertia | The tendency of an object to keep doing what it is doing: stay still, or keep moving in a straight line at the same speed |
| Item bank | A large pool of test questions from which each candidate's set is drawn at random |
| Lever | A rigid bar that turns about a pivot. A small force far from the pivot can balance a large force close to it. |
| Mechanical advantage | How many times a machine multiplies your force. It always costs extra distance. |
| Moment, or torque | Turning effect: force x distance from the pivot |
| Norm group | The comparison group your score is measured against, chosen by the employer |
| Ohm | The unit of electrical resistance |
| Ohm's law | Current = voltage / resistance |
| Parallel circuit | A circuit with separate branches. Each branch has its own path to the battery. |
| Percentile | The share of the norm group you scored the same as or higher than |
| Pitch, of a screw | How far a screw moves forward in one full turn |
| Pitch, of a sound | How high or low a note sounds. Faster vibration gives a higher pitch. |
| Pivot, or fulcrum | The point a lever turns about |
| Pressure | Force spread over an area. In a liquid it grows with depth. |
| Radiation | Heat travelling as rays, like sunlight. Dark, dull surfaces absorb it best. |
| Raw score | The number of items answered correctly |
| Refraction | Light changing direction as it passes from one material into another, such as water to air |
| Resistance | How much a part of a circuit opposes current, measured in ohms |
| Series circuit | A circuit with one single path. A break anywhere stops the current everywhere. |
| Siphon | A full tube that carries liquid over a rim from a higher surface to a lower one |
| Stanine | A nine-point standard score, 1 to 9, with 5 in the middle |
| Sten | A ten-point standard score, 1 to 10, with 5.5 in the middle |
| T-score | A standard score with a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10 |
| Theta score | Pearson's ability estimate for item-banked tests. It allows for the difficulty of each item you answered. |
| Voltage | The electrical push from a battery or supply, measured in volts (V) |
| Work | Force x distance moved. Simple machines reduce force but not work. |
