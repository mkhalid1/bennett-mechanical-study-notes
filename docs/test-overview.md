# The Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test explained

The Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test, now in its second edition as the BMCT-II, is a timed test of how well you understand the way physical things work: levers, gears, pulleys, liquids, heat, electricity and motion. Employers use it to screen people for technical and hands-on roles. This page covers what the publisher says about it, how it is built and what it is not.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test, second edition (BMCT-II) |
| Publisher | Pearson TalentLens |
| Questions | 55 items |
| Time | 25 minutes, timed |
| Delivery | Online. Can be taken remotely and unsupervised. |
| Device | Pearson recommends a desktop or laptop with a dependable internet connection, not a phone |
| Question pool | Items are drawn at random from an item bank, so no two candidates get the same set |
| Pictures | Pearson says questions come with "numerous visuals" |
| Result | An ability (theta) score, turned into a percentile against a norm group |
| Pass mark | None published. Each employer decides what it needs. |
| Languages | Dutch, English (UK and US), Spanish, French |

Source: [Pearson TalentLens, Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test (BMCT-II)](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html), checked 24 November 2026.

55 items in 25 minutes works out at about 27 seconds per item. That is the single most important number on this page: see [timing and pacing](timing.md).

## What it measures

Pearson says the BMCT-II was developed to measure:

- the ability to solve problems
- the ability to learn new mechanical concepts quickly
- the level of knowledge in basic mechanics
- mechanical reasoning and spatial perception

In plain terms, it checks whether you can look at a picture of a machine or a physical situation and predict what will happen. It is about understanding, not about reciting formulas. A few items involve simple arithmetic, such as a ratio or a multiplication, but the hard part is knowing which rule applies.

## How the test is built

Because every candidate gets a different set of items, Pearson has to keep the sets fair. According to its FAQ:

- Every item is coded by **difficulty**, on a scale from easy to high, and each test is built with the same number of items in each difficulty band.
- Every item is also coded by **content**, with Pearson naming electricity, heat and hydraulics as examples, and each test includes a set amount of each item type.
- Pearson's 2025 French product sheet says the test can assess **12 mechanical domains**, naming forces, acoustics and hydraulics as examples ([Pearson TalentLens, BMCT-II sheet, 2025](https://www.talentlens.com/content/dam/school/global/Global-Talentlens/fr/documents-solutions/Pearson-TalentLens-BMCT-2025.pdf)).
- Tests can still differ slightly in overall difficulty, and the scoring system allows for that.

Pearson's public pages do not list all 12 domains or how many items each one gets. You will find detailed splits quoted on prep sites, but we could not confirm them on a Pearson page, so we do not repeat them here. The eight topics in these notes (levers, gears, pulleys and ramps, fluids, heat, electricity, motion, and sound, light and shapes) are our own grouping of the physical principles behind those domains. They include every area Pearson names.

## Who takes it

Pearson lists three contexts of use: **recruitment, training and risk management**. It says the test is suitable for many sectors, naming maintenance, production and training, and that mechanical reasoning matters in fields such as **engineering, airlines, emergency services and skilled trades**. It also says the test predicts success in technical training, and that it can be used before and after a training course to measure what trainees learned.

In practice, you are most likely to meet it when applying for:

- maintenance and repair technician roles
- production and manufacturing roles
- apprenticeships and technical training programmes
- roles that involve operating or servicing equipment

Some colleges also run the test in person at their testing centres. Ozarks Technical Community College, for example, [lists the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test II](https://ozarkstech.edu/offered-exams/bennett-wonderlic/) with a 25-minute exam time, pre-registration, and a photo ID on the day.

## Versions you may hear about

The Bennett test has a long history. Pearson says it has been in use for more than 60 years. Older paper versions, known as Form S and Form T, are listed in some university test libraries, such as the [University of Plymouth's psychometric test listing](https://www.psy.plymouth.ac.uk/PsychometricTests/Test.aspx?TestId=515), which gives an administration time of 30 minutes. The [Buros Center for Testing](https://marketplace.unl.edu/buros/bennett-mechanical-comprehension-test-second-edition.html) describes the test's purpose as measuring "the ability to perceive and understand the relationship of physical forces and mechanical elements in practical situations."

If your invitation comes from an employer today, expect the online BMCT-II described in the quick facts above. Older guides that quote different question counts or times are usually describing the paper forms.

## What the questions look like

Each item shows a picture or diagram and asks a short question about it, such as "Which gear turns fastest?" or "Which bulb lights?". Pearson's product page does not publish the number of answer options per item. The practice items in these notes use three options, A, B and C, which is enough to teach the method.

Questions in the real test are drawn from Pearson's item bank and are not public. Every item in this repo is original.

## What it is not

- **Not a knowledge test of a trade.** You will not be asked to name tools or quote regulations. The items are about principles.
- **Not a maths test.** Arithmetic is light. Understanding which way something moves or which way a force acts matters far more.
- **Not something to cram from leaked questions.** Because the test is drawn from an item bank, memorised questions are unlikely to match yours. The method transfers. The answers do not.
