# FAQ

Questions people really ask about the Bennett test, taken from Google's "People also ask" results for US searches in November 2026 (see [sources](sources.md#search-data)), plus a few we are asked often. Facts about the test come from the publisher, Pearson TalentLens, and are linked.

## What is the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test?

A timed test of mechanical reasoning and spatial perception, published by Pearson TalentLens. The current version, the BMCT-II, has 55 picture-based items in 25 minutes and is taken online. Employers use it to screen candidates for technical and hands-on roles, and training providers use it to predict who will do well on technical courses ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)). See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## What does mechanical aptitude mean?

Your natural feel for how physical things work: which way a gear turns, why a long spanner helps, where water pressure is highest. It is partly learned from experience with tools and machines, and partly reasoning. Tests like the Bennett measure it by asking you to predict what happens in pictures of everyday mechanisms.

## How many questions is the Bennett test?

The BMCT-II has 55 items ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)). Older paper versions had different lengths, which is why some guides quote other numbers.

## How long is the Bennett test?

25 minutes, timed, for the BMCT-II ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)). That is about 27 seconds per item. See [timing and pacing](timing.md).

## Is the Bennett test hard?

The physics is not advanced: most items rest on one everyday rule. What makes it hard is the pace, the mix of topics, and the trap answers that "feel" right, such as heavier objects falling faster. People who have learned the rules and practised under time pressure find it much more manageable.

## Is it hard to pass a mechanical aptitude test?

There is usually no fixed pass mark. You are compared with other candidates or a norm group, and the employer sets the level it needs. That means "passing" depends on the role. The best way to improve your chances is to learn the core rules for each topic and practise timed. The [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) is a good structure.

## What is a good score on the Bennett test?

Pearson reports results as an ability score turned into a percentile against a norm group the employer chooses, and does not publish a pass mark ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)). A good score is one that meets your employer's cut-off, which you usually will not know. As a study target, aim for around 80% on timed practice. That is our guidance, not a published standard. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## Is there a pass mark?

Not a published one. Each employer decides the level it needs, often as a percentile against a norm group it picks. Numbers quoted online as "the" Bennett pass mark are not from Pearson.

## How do I study for the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test?

1. Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) to find your weak topics.
2. Work through the guide for each weak topic: learn the rule, then do the practice items.
3. Take a full timed practice test and review every miss.
4. Fix your weakest topics, then take a second timed run.

The [study plans](study-plans.md) set this out day by day.

## How do I pass a mechanical reasoning test?

Learn the small number of rules that most items rest on: turning effect, gear direction and ratios, counting pulley ropes, pressure with depth, series and parallel circuits, and inertia. Then practise recognising each type of item quickly, because the time limit is tight. Answer every item, since there is no published penalty for wrong answers on the BMCT-II.

## What are the names of some common mechanical aptitude tests?

Besides the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test, you may meet the Ramsay Corporation's mechanical and maintenance tests, the Wiesen Test of Mechanical Aptitude, and the mechanical comprehension part of the ASVAB, the US military's entrance test. They differ in length and format, but they draw on the same physical principles, so the topic guides in these notes help with all of them.

## Can I take the Bennett test on my phone?

Pearson recommends taking the BMCT-II on a desktop or laptop with a dependable internet connection ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)). The pictures are easier to read on a larger screen too.

## Does everyone get the same questions?

No. Pearson draws each candidate's items at random from an item bank, so no two applicants get the same set. Each set has the same balance of difficulty and content, and the scoring allows for small differences ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)).

## Can I use a calculator?

Pearson's product page does not say. Follow the instructions in your invitation. The arithmetic in the test is light, such as simple ratios and multiplication, so practise doing it in your head or on scrap paper.

## In which languages is it available?

Dutch, English (UK and US), Spanish and French ([Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/bennett-mechanical.html)).

## Can I retake the Bennett test?

That is up to the employer or programme. Pearson does not publish a candidate retake rule. Because you would get a different set of items, a retake is not the same test twice.
